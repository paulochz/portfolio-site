Overview

GPC is a system developed under the PRR (Recovery and Resilience Plan) to digitalize and automate the debt collection process at Social Security. The project replaced an entirely manual process, scattered across multiple subsystems and tools, with an integrated workflow within the Segurança Social Direta platform.

The problem

The problem

Before GPC, Social Security employees responsible for managing contribution debts worked entirely manually, relying on Excel files, paper, email, and various non-integrated subsystems. This resulted in a slow, error-prone process that was difficult to monitor.

Results and impact

10+ → 1 Ecosystem Consolidation

Unification of 10+ legacy subsystems into a single, interoperable interface.

< 5 min Resolution Time

Drastic reduction in employee task execution time (from several days to 5 minutes).

+700k Reach & Adoption

Operational Efficiency & Back-office

From 10+ Subsystems to 1 Platform, from days to 5 Minutes

Streamlining 10+ legacy subsystems into a single, interoperable interface was far more than an engineering milestone, it was driven by end-to-end Service Design and Employee Journey mapping.

How Design drove the Impact:

  • Uncovering Redundancies: In-depth mapping of the internal workflow exposed data fragmentation and repetitive manual inputs across legacy systems.
  • Unified Architecture: Designed a seamless, single-entry workflow that eliminated cross-tool friction and contextual context-switching.
  • Operational Impact: Drastically cut internal task completion time from several days down to just 5 minutes, multiplying team output and response speed.

Citizen Impact & Front-end

700,000+ Notifications Read on 1st Dispatch, reduced support overhead

Reaching over 700,000 citizens on their very first notification was more than a communication success, it was the direct outcome of turning bureaucratic jargon into an intuitive, action-driven user experience.

How Design Drove the Impact:

  • Plain Language Adoption: Replaced complex legal and administrative terminology with clear, empathetic messaging, actionable steps, and transparent deadline indicators.
  • Frictionless Navigation: Restructured the Information Architecture to remove nested menu barriers, establishing a direct, 1-click pathway from the main dashboard straight to notifications and actions.
  • Civic and Operational Impact: The high initial read rate reduced user anxiety, minimized follow-up inquiries, and significantly lightened the workload on support channels and helpdesks.

The project

GPC was structured into two main modules, developed in parallel throughout the project:

Contribution Notifications

Massive delivery of electronic notifications to Employers (EE) and Self-Employed Workers (TI).
Made available directly on the Segurança Social Direta platform.
Replacement of physical mail delivery.

Claims Module

Registration and consultation of claims by both taxpayers and internal employees.
Management of claim requests by the institutional user.
Full traceability of actions and owners.

Research process

I conducted five research cycles throughout the project, covering both internal users (Segurança Social staff) and external users (taxpayers). Each cycle directly informed the design of subsequent iterations.

Cycle 1
Exploratory research

Participants Profile Sessions
5 Self-employed and employer reps Moderated, remote

Before any design decision was made, it was essential to understand how the process actually worked in practice. I conducted remote interviews with 8 staff members from 5 district centres (Setúbal, Aveiro, Viseu, Madeira and Açores), selected based on their volume of criminal proceedings over the previous 5 years. Sessions ran with 1 to 2 participants simultaneously, with 2 moderators each: one facilitator and one observer.

Key findings

The most cited theme was dependency on tools and subsystems (21 mentions), followed by manual and time-consuming processes (10) and debt maps (8)

The full process spanned 10 manual stages, from selecting taxpayers to submitting to the internal system responsible for managing criminal infraction processes

Building the debt map was described as the hardest step, especially managing the different responsibility periods of each company director

Complete absence of traceability: no log of dates, actions, or responsible parties

User journey map

To map the full picture of the existing process, I built a user journey covering all 10 stages, from taxpayer selection to criminal report submission. The map documents the tools used at each step, the actions performed, the emotions experienced, and the pain points identified across sessions.

Cycle 2
Usability tests with external users (taxpayers)

Participants Profile Sessions
5 Self-employed and employer reps Moderated, remote

Based on the findings from Cycle 1 and the project requirements defined with the business team, I designed the first version of the contributions notifications flow, which served as the basis for the next round of user testing.

Key findings

4 out of 5 participants did not access the notification via the message inbox; they preferred the account statement, as the entry point did not match their mental model

80% were unsure whether the value shown was the total amount owed

60% did not immediately understand the “read date” field

Absence of a notification status indicator (resolved or pending) caused confusion

User quotes

“Read date? I don’t know if it’s when they opened the message or when the debt was generated.”

“I get the email from Social Security, but it doesn’t say whose it is. I have to investigate client by client.”

Cycle 3: Usability tests with ISS staff (contributions and complaints)

Participants Profile Sessions
15 Staff members Moderated, remote

The largest testing cycle in the project. I tested with 15 staff members across diverse profiles, including front desk, debt analysis, benefits, support, and 360 View, to evaluate the notifications flow and the new complaints functionality.

Key findings

Reliance on multiple subsystems without effective integration (incluiding spreadsheets and paper).

Manual and error-prone processes: creating debt sheets, tracking deadlines, and sending notifications by mail.

Lack of traceability: no logs of dates, actions, or owners responsible for the process.

Ineffective communication with taxpayers: incorrect payments, lack of proof of payment, and no clear instructions.

Overburdened with limited human resources: regional centers manually prioritized higher-value cases.

User quotes

“We need IT to work for us, not us working for IT.”

“Administrative offenses are a lot of work because they are done manually.”

Cycle 4: Usability tests with staff (complaints, second iteration)

Participants Profile Sessions
5 Staff members Moderated, remote

Following improvements to the flow, I returned to test with staff who manage complaints, focusing on the menu's information architecture and the process of registering a complaint on behalf of a taxpayer.

Key findings

Placing “Notifications” under “Payments and debt” caused immediate confusion

3 of 5 participants expected to be redirected to the complaints list after submitting a complaint

The statuses “Registered” and “Under review” were not sufficiently clear

Participants raised concerns about any staff member being able to cancel a complaint without supporting documentation

User quotes

“Payments means something I’ve already done, and debt is something I haven’t paid.”

“I wouldn’t put notifications inside payments.”

Cycle 5: Usability tests with staff (contributions)

Participants Profile Sessions
5 Staff members Moderated, remote

The most recent cycle tested the new contribution debt notifications flow with 5 staff members specialised in criminal proceedings.

Key findings

Navigation and the tab separation (contributions / contributions) were well received: 4 out of 5 reached the screen without difficulty

Failures occurred at reading moments: error messages, button labels, and missing context

3 out of 5 participants independently flagged the absence of the employer’s NISS on the MOE notification detail screen as a critical gap

The buttons “Get notification” and “Get receipt” were not immediately distinguished by 2 out of 5 participants

Users with established habits in the previous system need support during the transition

User quotes

“I found the navigation between tabs intuitive.”

“This new Documents tab is a real improvement.”

“It’s more accessible than it was before. The notifications used to be hidden behind payments, now they’re more visible.”

Key design decisions

Language as the central problem

Across all five testing cycles, the dominant failure pattern was not navigation. It was language. Technical terms, ambiguous labels, and messages with no guidance were responsible for most errors and user frustration.

A three-part UX writing framework was applied across all interface copy: what happened, why it happened, and what to do next. Sentences averaged 15 to 20 words, in plain language with no technical jargon. All terminology was validated against legal and professional sources before being finalised. A clear distinction was maintained throughout between 'contributions' and 'contributions', two parallel categories with different audiences and legal implications.

Error message rewrite
Before

"No results found."

After

"The number entered belongs to the contributions scope. Go to the Contributions tab to find this notification."

Information architecture: the menu as a barrier

Across three separate cycles (January 2025, August 2025, April 2026), users familiar with the previous system failed to find the correct entry point because they followed memorised paths that no longer worked. Placing 'Notifications' inside 'Payments and debt' was challenged in multiple sessions. Users mentally distinguish between what they have already paid and what they still owe, and the menu hierarchy did not reflect this mental model.

Proposed solution

Create a top-level 'Notifications' menu item at the same level as 'Payments and debt', with a contextual signpost during the transition period for users of the previous system.

Context continuity across screens

In the contribution flow, users searched using the employer's NISS to find MOE notifications, but once inside the detail screen, that reference disappeared. Users lost the thread of their own search.

Proposed fix

Display the employer's NISS and name in the header of the MOE notification detail screen, keeping the search context visible throughout the consultation.

Learnings

What worked well

Starting with exploratory research before any design decision meant solutions were grounded in real user pain, not team assumptions

Running testing cycles continuously throughout development allowed problems to be caught and addressed iteration by iteration

The user journey map built in August 2024 became a shared reference artefact used by the team throughout the entire project

The tab separation of contributions and contributions was validated as intuitive and effective by users from both profiles

What I would do differently

Invest earlier in terminology consistency: language problems appeared in every cycle and could have been anticipated with a shared glossary from the start

Involve external users (taxpayers) in more testing cycles, as the majority of sessions were conducted with internal users

Document design decisions more systematically, including why certain alternatives were discarded, to ease context handover to new team members
