Context and business challenge

Yamaha Financial Services was launching a new financial vertical focused on enabling customers to finance Yamaha vehicles, including motorbikes and boats. The initiative required building trust as a financial services provider while simplifying a credit application process that was previously complex and inefficient.

The product needed to serve two audiences simultaneously. Customers required a clear and accessible way to apply for credit, including individuals without traditional income documentation. Internal teams required operational efficiency to review, process, and manage applications.

I led the UX and UI design from early discovery through delivery, collaborating closely with product, engineering, and stakeholders to define a scalable and accessible digital experience.

Project context

Project scope predefined, no direct user data

The project scope was predefined before our involvement. Delivery timelines were fixed and access to end users was limited. Because of this, decisions relied on competitive analysis, domain expertise in financial services, stakeholder workshops, and established usability principles.

The challenge was to design a product that balanced regulatory clarity, operational efficiency, and brand alignment without direct user interviews or usability testing with end customers.

The problem

Yamaha needed a dedicated website for its sub-brand, Yamaha Financial Services. The existing product was not intuitive, visually cohesive, or user-friendly enough to attract and retain its target audience.

The objective was to transform the experience into a more intuitive, recognizable, and visually consistent platform that simplified access to loan information while also supporting efficient internal operations for Yamaha employees.

Existing solution

The existing platform presented several issues that limited adoption and efficiency:
  • Navigation was fragmented and difficult to understand
  • Visual inconsistencies weakened brand perception
  • The mobile experience did not reflect the volume of mobile traffic
  • Information hierarchy lacked clarity in multi-step forms
  • Accessibility standards were not consistently applied
  • Internal workflows required unnecessary manual steps

These issues affected both application completion rates and internal processing efficiency.

Goals

The redesign focused on five primary objectives:

Simplify the credit application journey

Reduce friction across multi-step forms and improve completion rates.

Support alternative income verification

Enable customers without traditional documentation to submit contextual proof and continue their application process.

Improve operational efficiency

Design tools that reduce manual review effort and accelerate processing time for internal teams.

Strengthen brand perception

Align the interface with Yamaha's premium positioning while maintaining clarity and trustworthiness in financial contexts.

Ensure accessibility and responsiveness

Apply accessibility best practices and responsive design standards across devices.

Content audit and structural redesign

Auditing the system and uncovering opportunities

Before initiating the visual redesign, we conducted a structured content inventory of the existing platform. This allowed us to identify gaps, redundancies, and opportunities for reorganization before committing to new design patterns.

Design approach

Given the limited access to end users, design decisions were guided by competitive benchmarking, established usability heuristics, and iterative stakeholder feedback.

1. Understanding the business and regulatory context

We analyzed Yamaha's brand guidelines, financial product requirements, and compliance constraints. Financial services require a careful balance between clarity, transparency, and trust.

2. Competitive benchmarking

I conducted a thorough analysis of competitors and industry trends, involving a detailed review of UX/UI designs from similar financial services platforms to identify patterns and opportunities.

3. Establishing a scalable design system

Angular Material served as a structural foundation, but we customized components to reflect Yamaha's visual identity and accessibility requirements.

4. Interaction and interface design

The application journey was redesigned using progressive disclosure principles to reduce cognitive load. Clear progress indicators and contextual assistance were introduced at each stage.

5. Stakeholder validation

Although direct end-user testing was not available, we conducted structured walkthrough sessions with stakeholders and operational teams to validate design decisions and refine interactions.

Visual language and accessibility

A new color palette was developed to balance brand alignment with accessibility requirements. Every combination was tested for WCAG 2.1 AA contrast compliance.

Key learnings

Design under constraints

Working within a predefined scope reinforced the importance of leveraging heuristics, competitive benchmarking, and stakeholder workshops as substitutes for direct user research.

Design systems as strategic assets

Investing in a structured design system early reduced rework, accelerated development cycles, and improved cross-team collaboration.

Balancing brand and clarity

Financial interfaces must communicate stability and transparency. Aligning premium brand elements with usability principles required ongoing iteration.
